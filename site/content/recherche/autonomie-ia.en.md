## The question

A lawyer who works with an AI tool delegates something. Sometimes the execution of a task they have mastered, sometimes the framing of the problem, sometimes the criteria for the choice, and sometimes, without noticing, the decision itself. This programme starts from one question: under what conditions do AI environments strengthen human capacities for understanding, judgment and action, rather than replace them?

## What the programme studies

- **Autonomy and delegation**: what is actually entrusted to the tool, and what is kept.
- **Effective oversight**: what it takes to detect an error, challenge it, correct it or refuse an answer, beyond simply “having it reviewed”.
- **Knowledge and learning**: what repeated use does to competence and to the ability to act without assistance.
- **Trust and automation bias**: when a plausible answer, a polished interface or the vocabulary used leads people to accept an error.
- **Organisation and agency**: whether time, targets and hierarchy really leave room to challenge the tool.
- **Responsibility and justification**: how to keep a reasoned decision whose author has made the reasoning their own.

## Where the programme stands

This is the laboratory's first programme, opened in November 2026. Its first published work is an AI governance toolkit, which makes verifiable the controls that a charter merely states, and reviews that confront the programme with what others have written. Three research papers are in preparation for the first half of 2027, on human oversight, the pricing of legal services and AI models in practice.
