## The question

Much of a law firm's knowledge is written down nowhere: how to negotiate a given clause, the thresholds below which a point is accepted, the exceptions known from experience.

AI tools can only rely on that knowledge once it has been made explicit. This programme asks how legal knowledge can be formalised and passed on without erasing the context that gives it meaning.

## What the programme studies

- **Making expertise explicit**: turning tacit knowledge into rules, negotiation playbooks or decision models.
- **Knowledge representation**: what rules, ontologies, graphs or probabilistic models can and cannot do.
- **Contract engineering**: representing preferences, thresholds, exceptions and trade-offs, for instance in an SME's contract policy.
- **Transmission and updating**: keeping track of the provenance, authority, version and territorial validity of a piece of knowledge.
- **Interoperability**: which knowledge and which actions to expose to AI tools or agents, and under what control.

## Where the programme stands

The programme is planned as an extension, to be opened when the work of the first programme allows it. No work has been published in it yet. A research paper in preparation for early 2027, on AI models in legal practice, will address some of its questions.
