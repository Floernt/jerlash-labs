The tools published here help you do a piece of work; they do not do it for you. This page brings together the rules that apply to all of them. Each tool page adds the precautions specific to that tool.

## Check every result

A tool can be wrong by omission (it misses an item) or by excess (it flags an item of no importance). A result with no alert means that the items searched for were not found, within the limits described on the tool page. It does not guarantee that the document is ready.

When a tool uses an AI model, its output may contain inaccurate statements, references that do not exist or omissions. Read it as you would any work delegated to a machine: carefully, and going back to the sources.

## Know where your documents go

Each tool page contains a “Data path” section that distinguishes three destinations: your computer, the AI provider if there is one, and jerlash.labs. For the last of these, the answer is always “nothing”: the site receives no documents, no keys and no usage data.

When a tool sends data to an AI provider, it does so with your own account. What the provider does with it is governed by your contract with them. Before passing documents covered by professional secrecy or personal data through it, check that contract: retention period, use for training, location, sub-processors. Compliance with professional secrecy and the GDPR remains your responsibility.

## Work on copies

jerlash.labs tools do not modify the file you give them. Nevertheless, keep the habit of working on a copy and leaving the original intact.

## Protect your reports and lists

The reports and lists the tools produce (extracts from documents, client names) are as confidential as the case files they are drawn from. Store them with the case file, not in a shared space.

## Keep the tools up to date

A corrected version may be published when a defect is discovered. The [Updates](/nouveautes/) page and its RSS feed announce it. A version with a known serious defect is withdrawn and flagged.

## Liability

The tools are provided free of charge, as is, without warranty, under the MIT licence. They do not constitute legal advice. Their use in a case file is a matter for the judgement of the lawyer using them.
