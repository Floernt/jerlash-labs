This text is the model for a “Charter for the governance and responsible use of artificial intelligence”. It is only a model. Each firm adapts it to its size, its uses and the rules that apply to it, then has it adopted by its competent body. The passages in square brackets are to be completed. The thresholds, weightings and frequencies to which the charter refers are set by each organisation, in its annexes.

The charter comes at the end of the process, not at the beginning. It decides nothing by itself: it records and anchors the choices the organisation has made in setting up [governance](../gouvernance/), classifying [data](../donnees/), setting the rules for [approval](../homologation/) and [oversight](../supervision/), and organising [operations](../operations/). A charter drafted before these choices remains a declaration of principles, with no hold on practice.

## Preamble

Artificial intelligence is changing the way legal professionals research, analyse, produce and act. It can improve the quality and efficiency of services. It also introduces risks: breaches of confidentiality, error, opacity, dependence and inappropriate automation.

The organisation adopts a principle of effective professional control. The formal presence of a human or a superficial review is not enough. Professionals must be able to understand the determining points of the outputs they use, check them, challenge them, correct them, refuse them and justify their choices. They must have real means of doing so.

## Article 1. Purpose, persons and systems covered

The charter governs the selection, purchase, development, configuration, experimentation, use, monitoring and discontinuation of AI systems.

It applies to partners, lawyers, trainees, legal staff, administrative teams and authorised contributors, to the extent of their commitments.

It covers stand-alone applications, features built into other software, including software already in place, augmented search engines, assistants, agents and automations that act on other services.

It never exempts anyone from complying with the law, professional secrecy, the rules of professional ethics, the rules of employment law or the commitments made to clients.

## Article 2. Principles

1. Every use meets an identified professional need (specified purpose).
2. AI exercises neither the lawyer's professional judgement nor their authority to advise or decide (judgement and independence).
3. Confidentiality, professional secrecy, security and personal data are assessed before any transmission (protection).
4. The intensity of checks depends on the potential impact, the sensitivity of the data and the autonomy of the system (proportionality).
5. Determining statements rest on verifiable elements, independent of the system's output alone (verifiability).
6. Checking presupposes competence, time, access to sources and the power to stop the operation (capacity to challenge).
7. Significant uses are documented, without creating excessive archives of confidential data (useful traceability).
8. Practice fosters the development of professional capabilities, not their systematic abandonment (maintaining competence).

## Article 3. Bodies and organisational responsibility

The management body adopts the policy and determines the tolerated level of risk. The AI lead keeps the register and coordinates evaluations. The security, data protection and professional ethics compliance functions take part according to their competences. The case file lead validates the analyses that commit their engagement. The user complies with authorisations to use and reports anomalies.

In a small organisation, a single body may combine several functions. A decision with significant stakes is nevertheless subject to a review independent of the person proposing it, or to a documented compensatory measure. The separation between promoting a tool, assessing its risk and deciding is proportionate to the size of the organisation.

The detailed rules are set out in the [governance](../gouvernance/) policy.

## Article 4. Approval of tools and uses

Approval covers a precise scope: the provider, the product, the model and its version, the configuration, the features, the connectors, the data categories, the tasks, the user categories, the level of autonomy and the duration of the authorisation.

It comprises:

1. eliminatory conditions;
2. a risk analysis;
3. a multi-criteria evaluation;
4. tests carried out in the situation of use;
5. a reasoned decision;
6. monitoring of changes.

Governance defines the approval framework, the weightings, any thresholds and the decision-making powers. An abstract list of “reliable” brands is no substitute for this.

The authorisation may be full within a scope, limited, conditional, experimental, suspended or refused. A change of purpose, data, connector, configuration or model may require a new evaluation.

Depending on the governance level chosen by the organisation, approval may be reduced to a compliance sheet for the tool. A tool whose compliance is not established, and which has not been approved, receives only public or non-sensitive internal data (classes D0 and D1), unless an experiment has been expressly authorised and is accompanied by appropriate safeguards.

The procedure is set out in the [approval](../homologation/) module.

## Article 5. Data and professional secrecy

Before entering data into a system, the user checks its sensitivity class, the purpose, the recipients and compatibility with the approval. Minimisation, pseudonymisation or the use of synthetic data are preferred where relevant. They are not always enough to discharge legal and professional ethics obligations.

The processing of personal data requires an adequate analysis of roles, lawful bases, information of individuals, contracts, retention periods, international transfers and security.

Data covered by professional secrecy require a specific analysis of human and technical access, of their reuse and of the contractual chain. Hosting in Europe is not, by itself, a sufficient guarantee.

The classification is set out in the [data](../donnees/) module.

## Article 6. Internal classification of use cases

The organisation classifies uses according to their possible effects. The charter takes up the classification adopted when [approval](../homologation/) was set up.

| Class | Title | Uses covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| U1 | Routine assistance | Formatting or rewording of public material, with little consequence. |
| U2 | Professional assistance | Summaries, research, drafts or analyses that require targeted verification. |
| U3 | Legal stakes | Deeds, advice, strategic choices, or situations where the rights of individuals may be affected. |
| U4 | Autonomous action or critical risk | Execution of external, irreversible, large-scale or hard-to-control actions. |

This classification gives a first assessment. Aggravating factors may lead to stronger checks.

These classes are internal to the organisation and are not to be confused with the categories of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. In the field of justice, the high-risk AI systems covered by that regulation concern the administration of justice, that is, the work of judges. The practice of a law firm or of legal professionals does not fall within it. One rule follows: no deviation from the intended purpose of the AI system used should be contemplated.

## Article 7. Effective human oversight

Depending on the use, the check must make it possible:

- to understand the task and the limits of the system;
- to verify the determining elements;
- to challenge the conclusions, assumptions and omissions;
- to correct the analysis;
- to refuse or interrupt an output or an action;
- to justify the reasons for accepting it.

A supervisor is effective only if they have sufficient competence, information, time and authority. A simple click on “approve”, with no realistic possibility of identifying or correcting an error, does not amount to oversight.

The control measures are defined in the operational annex, see [oversight](../supervision/). Conflicts of interest and the organisation's constraints are given particular consideration there.

## Article 8. Use and validation of outputs

All information or output is subject to the level of validation that corresponds to its use. Validation may cover the accuracy of the facts, whether the law is up to date, the authenticity and relevance of sources, the reasoning, the facts of the case file, uncertainties, third-party rights and the quality of the presentation. For determining legal statements, references are consulted in their source, and not accepted because they seem plausible.

Any output used in a service is understood and validated to the degree required by its nature, its intended use and its risks. Copying an output word for word, rewriting it, translating it or reorganising it is in itself neither a guarantee of reliability nor a sufficient indication of it.

External documents with legal stakes are validated by a competent lawyer, subject to the rules specific to the organisation and to the professions concerned.

## Article 9. Agents and external actions

Any system that reads, modifies, sends, files, signs, pays or triggers an action is authorised only within a specified scope of actions, with permissions reduced to the minimum.

The interfaces, connectors, recipients, volumes, logs, limits and stop mechanisms are specified.

Depending on the risk, actions that commit the organisation or are difficult to undo are subject to prior validation, to reinforced technical controls, or to both.

Automating a workflow does not amount to authorising each of the acts that make it up.

## Article 10. Transparency, traceability and retention

For each critical use, it must be possible to retrieve at least the tool and its configuration, the task, the determining human intervention, the relevant sources or checks, and the decision to publish or execute.

The precision of records, access to logs and the retention period depend on the risk and the applicable obligations. The organisation avoids systematically recording instructions (prompts) and documents containing secrets where such retention is not necessary.

The obligation to inform clients and third parties is assessed according to the applicable law, the engagement and the nature of the AI's involvement. No mechanical rule applies uniformly to all tasks.

The procedures are set out in the [operations](../operations/) module.

## Article 11. Relations with clients, courts and third parties

The firm never presents content, or the level of verification it has undergone, in a misleading manner.

Where required, it examines the information or consent of the client, contractual commitments and procedural rules.

Outputs intended for courts, administrations, opposing parties or clients comply with the professional ethics and professional obligations specific to their destination.

## Article 12. Allocation of responsibilities

The use of AI removes none of the responsibilities arising from the law and from professional commitments.

The organisation assumes its obligations of governance, resources and security. The case file lead retains control of their engagement. The user complies with authorised uses and applicable checks. Providers are liable according to the contracts and the law in force.

This internal allocation affects neither the rights of third parties nor the legal rules on liability.

## Article 13. Training, authorisation to use and maintaining capabilities

Users receive training suited to their role, the system and the use case. Activities with significant stakes may be subject to an authorisation to use or to a test.

The training programme covers plausible errors, automation bias, source verification, confidentiality, oversight techniques, agents, incidents and the ability to stop a system.

A review, at the frequency set by the organisation, ensures that essential professional skills continue to be exercised.

## Article 14. Incidents and escalation

Any incident, near miss or suspicion of disclosure, serious error, prohibited use or unexpected action is reported without undue delay, through the designated channel.

The AI lead coordinates the qualification of the incident, the limitation of its effects, the technical and legal analyses, any notification obligations and the correction. A precautionary suspension may be decided, in accordance with the defined competences.

No uniform notification deadline is presumed to apply to all incidents.

## Article 15. Monitoring and reassessment

The organisation monitors the functioning of systems, incidents, the quality of outputs, changes and competences.

It reassesses approvals at the frequency it sets. It reviews its governance after any major incident or change in the applicable law.

Checks concerning staff comply with the rules of employment law, confidentiality and data protection.

## Article 16. Adoption and revision

The charter enters into force on the date of its adoption.

The authorised body keeps the operational annexes up to date, within the limits of the approved principles. A substantive revision of the charter follows the governance procedure.

Each version includes a history and is communicated to the persons concerned.

## What the process must have produced before adoption

The charter is adopted only once these elements have been determined or gathered, mostly in the course of the previous modules:

- the legal owner of the charter;
- the applicable rules of professional ethics;
- the body competent to adopt it;
- the AI lead and their deputy;
- consultation of workers, where applicable;
- the appeal procedure;
- the retention policy;
- the security measures;
- the contractual documents;
- the review schedule;
- the status of the annexes.
