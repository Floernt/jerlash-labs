This page sets out which texts the toolkit rests on, the date at which the law was taken into account and what has not yet been checked.

## Cut-off date for the law

Cut-off date for the law for this version: November 2026.

Version 0.1 stated that external checks had been stopped on 2 November 2026. The list that follows guides documentary research. It does not constitute a complete compliance review, either under Belgian law or in a cross-border situation.

## Texts and references cited

### Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 on artificial intelligence

In the field of justice, the high-risk AI systems covered by Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 concern the administration of justice, that is, the work of judges. The practice of a law firm or of legal professionals does not fall within it. One rule follows: no deviation from the intended purpose of the AI system used should be contemplated.

The regulation was amended in 2026.

The toolkit's internal levels (U1 to U4 for uses, S1 to S4 for checks, D0 to D4 for data) are organisational tools. They are distinct from the categories of the regulation.

### Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR)

Text: [eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj).

### CCBE guide on generative AI

Council of Bars and Law Societies of Europe (CCBE), *Guide on the use of generative AI for lawyers*, 2025: [PDF document on ccbe.eu](https://www.ccbe.eu/fileadmin/speciality_distribution/public/documents/IT_LAW/ITL_Guides_recommendations/EN_ITL_20251002_CCBE-guide-on-the-use-of-the-use-of-generative-AI-for-lawyers.pdf). It is a useful professional reference. It does not have the force of Belgian law.

### Opinion 28/2024 of the European Data Protection Board

European Data Protection Board (EDPB), Opinion 28/2024 on certain data protection aspects related to the processing of personal data in the context of AI models: [edpb.europa.eu](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/opinion-of-the-board-art-64/opinion-282024-on-certain-data-protection-aspects-related-to_en).

## What remains to be checked

Before an organisation adopts the toolkit, a Belgian legal review must cover the following points.

- The rules of professional ethics of the Bar concerned: professional secrecy, digital infrastructure, relations with the client, duty of care, control of acts.
- Data protection: lawful basis, processing on behalf of the controller, controllership, rights of individuals, possible need for a data protection impact assessment (DPIA), access to data by service providers and transfers.
- Information of clients. A uniform obligation to disclose every instance of drafting assistance should not be presumed: the contract, professional ethics, data protection law and the transparency rules specific to the sector need to be examined.
- Monitoring of staff: employment law, consultation of representatives and proportionality of activity logs.
- Intellectual property in instructions (*prompts*), corpora and outputs, third-party rights and possible reuses.

## Rules followed for sources

Each version states the cut-off date for the law, the jurisdictions covered and the assumptions made. A link to a source does not replace checking the relevant provision. A technical or professional ethics opinion is not presented as an obligation of positive law without an identified basis. If a text or case law changes, a corrected version or an erratum is published.
