Artificial intelligence is stirring up the legal world, and solutions are multiplying. But before choosing a piece of software, you still need to know what you want to do with it. What if the real transformation began… with a blank sheet and a few Post-its? A simple method for moving from a passing fad to a clear, concrete and genuinely useful strategy.

--- 

For a few months now, offerings have been springing up everywhere: legal AI for research, drafting, contract management, client relations... Vendors compete with promises and impressive demos. And in many firms, the temptation is strong to “do something” with AI, and quickly.

But for partners who run their firm like a business, the real issue lies elsewhere. The challenge is not to choose between two tools. **The challenge is knowing what you want to transform**. And for that, a matrix is sometimes worth more than a benchmark.

**The reflex that costs us time (and money)**

When a firm decides to “equip itself with AI”, it often starts with the most visible part: the solution. We compare, we listen to sales reps, we ask fellow lawyers, we try to work out whether this software is “better” than that one.

But this reflex – starting from the tools – traps us. 

First because it assumes that we already know what we want to automate. 

Then because it prevents us from thinking of the transformation as a process – gradual, iterative, collective – rather than as a box to tick.

AI will not solve your organisational problems if you have not identified them. 

It will not generate value if you do not know where your value lies. And it will never be adopted if your teams cannot see what it changes, concretely, for them.

**Taking back control, before plugging in**

So where to begin? Certainly not with a feature checklist. 

Start with a simple question: “In our firm, what takes up our time without any real added value?”

This question, asked seriously, can become a tremendous strategic lever. And it deserves better than an improvised round-table or a brainstorming session. 

It deserves a method. A framework. A matrix.

**The matrix: a low-tech tool for prioritising AI**

Picture a board, a few Post-its, an hour of quiet. You are a partner in a firm of ten, thirty or a hundred people. You invite your associates, perhaps your support staff. Not to talk about “AI” in general, but to identify what slows you down day to day.

Everyone writes down ideas, repetitive tasks. Without filtering, without judging. Then these tasks are placed in a two-axis matrix:

– **Recurrence** (on the horizontal axis): how often does this task come up?  
– **Value** (on the vertical axis): how important is it to the firm – in financial, strategic or emotional terms?

The tasks in the top right – those that are both frequent and high-value – are your priorities. They are the best candidates for automation or optimisation through AI.

But beware: just because a task is a priority does not mean it should be tackled first. To get an AI initiative started, it is often better to begin with something simpler…

**Bet on small wins to build momentum**

In every firm, there are one or two tasks that tick every box: tedious, recurring and high-stakes. They are quickly spotted on the matrix. But they are rarely the best place to start. Too complex, too sensitive, or simply too long to transform.

To get an AI initiative going, it is better to aim for a “quick win”. A modest but visible task. A simple improvement, achievable in a few days, with a concrete impact. 

Generating drafts of LinkedIn posts. 
Replying to standard emails. 
Extracting data from court decisions. 
Summarising long documents.

The aim? To show that it works. To build trust. To bring teams on board gently. And above all, to make AI a lever, not a fantasy.

Because once this first success is achieved, everything changes. AI is no longer a project apart. It becomes a collective skill. 

And then you can tackle the substantive issues: production, client relations, legal analysis.

**AI is not a magic wand. It is a culture to be cultivated.**

Integrating artificial intelligence into a law firm is not adopting one more tool. It means agreeing to look at your organisation differently. Asking yourself what really matters. Delegating, little by little, what does not deserve the attention of a human brain.

This path rarely begins with a great technological upheaval. It often begins with a calm discussion, a few Post-its, and a sincere question: “What could we do differently?”

The good news is that this approach is within everyone’s reach. It requires neither an outsized budget nor an AI expert. Just a little method, curiosity… and clear-sightedness.

So, if you had to bring in one tool this year, start with a matrix. The rest will follow.
